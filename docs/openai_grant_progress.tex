\section{Overview}
This document records what has been built toward the OpenAI Metascience proposal and
how to reach each piece. In short: we extended the commercial-potential measure
(Masclans, Hasan \& Cohen 2025) into a \textbf{family of ``potential'' models}, built a
reusable model factory to train them, deployed them behind an API, and added two
Claude-powered surfaces --- a research agent and an interactive ecosystem explorer.

Everything is grounded in the existing Scientifiq data: $\sim$30M publications with
abstracts and potential scores, OpenAlex works and their citation graph, USPTO
PatentsView (assignees, CPC classes), and Reliance-on-Science (paper$\rightarrow$patent
citations), all in the project's BigQuery.

\section{The three new predictive models}
Each new model is the direct analog of commercial potential --- where that measure is
``the predicted likelihood a \emph{renewed patent} cites the article,'' each new head
swaps in a different, observable downstream label. All are trained the same way
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) and served from one endpoint (Section~\ref{sec:access}).

\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Label (what ``positive'' means)} & \textbf{Val.\ AUROC} \\
\midrule
Defense Impact & Cited by a patent assigned to a defense entity (prime, national lab, or government/defense body). & \textbf{0.897} \\
Complex-Invention & Cited by a patent whose CPC classification spans $\geq 3$ distinct sections --- a genuinely multi-domain invention. & \textbf{0.874} \\

\noindent Alongside the pre-existing \textbf{commercial}, \textbf{scientific}, and
\textbf{social} potential scores, every paper can now be described by a six-dimensional
\emph{impact fingerprint}.

\section{Method: a model factory}\label{sec:method}
The models share one lean pipeline, packaged as \texttt{sciscore} (a small Python
library in the repository under \texttt{ml/}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Label} in BigQuery via weak supervision --- the ``positive'' condition is
  observed in data we already hold (funding, patent citations, CPC classes, cross-field
  citations). No hand annotation. Each label query is $\sim$\$0.30--\$1.20 to run.
  \item \textbf{Embed} title + abstract with a frozen SciBERT encoder (mean-pooled, 768-dim).
  \item \textbf{Fit} a lightweight linear head on the frozen vectors (balanced, $\sim$10k rows).
  \item \textbf{Serve} every trained task from one API.
\end{enumerate}
Deliberately lean: frozen embeddings + a linear head, no per-year model family, no
calibration ceremony. Simulations and our results indicate $\sim$10k rows suffice.
\textbf{Adding a new model is a new YAML file plus a training run} --- see
Section~\ref{sec:ideas}.

\section{How to reach everything}\label{sec:access}
API keys and superadmin access will be granted separately; the paths below are stable.

\subsection{The scoring API (all models)}
Hosted on Google Cloud Run (project \texttt{com-sci-2}, scales to zero).
\begin{lstlisting}[style=sh]
Base URL:  https://sciscore-1029497676656.us-central1.run.app

GET  /health          liveness
GET  /tasks           -> {"tasks":["defense_impact","complex_invention","interdisciplinary"]}
POST /score           body {"task":"<name>","text":"<abstract>"}
                      header  Authorization: Bearer <SCISCORE_API_KEY>
                      -> {"task":"...","score":0.0-1.0,"stars":1-5}
\end{lstlisting}
Example:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=sh]
curl -X POST https://sciscore-1029497676656.us-central1.run.app/score \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <SCISCORE_API_KEY>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"task":"defense_impact","text":"<paper abstract>"}'
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Defense Impact --- in the application}
Defense scoring is deliberately \textbf{superadmin-gated} in the Superadditive app
(the account will be shared with named people).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Module:} \url{https://superadditive.app/start/defense-impact} --- paste an
  abstract (and optionally a DOI); returns a score, the defense domains it touches,
  translation pathways, and, when a DOI is given, the \emph{real} defense-assigned
  patents that already cite the paper.
  \item \textbf{Report:} rendered at \texttt{/defense/<code>} after a run.
  \item \textbf{Public API:} \texttt{POST /api/v1/defense-impact} with header
  \texttt{Authorization: Bearer <DEFENSE\_API\_KEY>} --- returns the model score plus an
  LLM-written read (domains, pathways, dual-use note, verdict) grounded in patent evidence.
\end{itemize}
The Defense Impact score is a research-\emph{mapping} measure --- a transparent,
uncertainty-aware lens on where science flows toward public and defense applications,
built only from public bibliometric signals. It maps relevance, not intent.

\subsection{Research Agent}
\url{https://superadditive.app/agent} (login required). One natural-language box over the
platform: it routes a question to the right capability --- \emph{find experts /
collaborators}, \emph{score an idea's potential}, or \emph{map a field} --- runs the
matching query over Scientifiq's data and models, and answers grounded only in the
retrieved evidence. Defense scoring is included only for superadmins.

\subsection{Ecosystem Explorer}
Inside the \emph{Domain Brief} module: enter a technology domain and scope (an institution,
a region, or global) and explore the collaboration network interactively --- click a
researcher for their profile and the cross-disciplinary bridges they should build, filter
by institution, and recolor the map by commercial / scientific / social potential to see
where value concentrates. Dashed links are suggested collaborations across structural holes.

\subsection{Batch scorer}
\url{https://superadditive.app/batch} (superadmin). Upload up to 50 abstracts --- a CSV with
an \texttt{abstract} column (and optional \texttt{id}/\texttt{doi}), or pasted text --- and
get every paper's six-dimensional impact fingerprint (commercial, scientific, social,
defense, complex-invention, interdisciplinary) in one pass, sortable and downloadable as CSV.
This is the ``score a portfolio'' workflow: surface the hidden gems in a lab's output, the
defense-relevant work in a department, or the cross-disciplinary bridges in a funder's awards.
The sciscore models score the whole batch in a single request each; the base scores run
concurrently. For a full corpus (thousands of papers) the same models are called directly
via the \texttt{/score} endpoint with a \texttt{texts} array.

\section{Reproducibility}
The repository contains everything to rebuild the models:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \texttt{ml/sciscore/} --- the training + inference + serving library.
  \item \texttt{ml/sql/} --- the BigQuery labeling queries for all three datasets.
  \item \texttt{ml/tasks/} --- one YAML per model (encoder, label column, sample size).
  \item \texttt{ml/models/} --- the trained heads (4\,KB each) baked into the Cloud Run image.
  \item \texttt{ml/notebooks/} --- a browser (Colab) training notebook, no local setup needed.
\end{itemize}
Rebuilding one model: run its \texttt{ml/sql} query to a CSV, then
\texttt{sciscore train --task ml/tasks/<name>.yaml --data <name>.csv --out models/<name>},
then redeploy.

\section*{Note on the Interdisciplinary model}
It is the weakest head (0.75). The label was sharpened once --- from ``cited by $\geq 3$
fields'' to ``a majority of citers outside the home field'' --- which helped modestly.
The honest read is that ex-ante interdisciplinary influence is hard to predict from an
abstract alone; a further refinement (weighting by citation distance across the field
hierarchy) is the natural next step.
